# US Airport Security & Immigration Wait Times

Minute-resolution TSA security checkpoint wait times for 30 US airports, plus
hourly CBP immigration hall wait times for arriving international passengers.

Airports publish their current wait time and then overwrite it. Nobody keeps the
history. This dataset is that history: a continuous archive collected by polling
each airport's public feed roughly once a minute.

Collection began **1 April 2026** with the New York, Philadelphia and Florida
airports; the rest were added later. **Coverage therefore starts on different**
**dates per airport** — see the From column below, and do not read a short history
as a quiet period.

As far as I know there is no other open historical dataset of US airport
checkpoint wait times.

Collected for, and used in production by, the live wait-time boards at
[nycwaittimes.com](https://nycwaittimes.com/) and sister sites. The archive is a
byproduct of running those services, which is why it keeps growing.

## Why `waits` rather than `waits_raw`

The scraper polls every ~60 seconds, but the upstream feeds refresh much more
slowly — so most consecutive scrapes re-record an identical reading. `waits`
collapses each run of unchanged readings into a single row spanning
`first_seen` → `last_seen`, with `n_scrapes` recording how many polls saw it.
That is 52% of the rows and none of the information.

Use `waits_raw` only if you specifically care about polling artifacts.

## Caveats — please read before drawing conclusions

**These are posted wait times, not measured transit times.** Every value is what
the airport chose to display. It is an operator's estimate, produced by methods
that differ per airport and are not published. Treat this as a dataset about
_what airports told passengers_, which may or may not match what passengers
experienced.

**Effective resolution is not one minute, and it varies by airport.** The
scraper polls every ~60s, but each operator refreshes on its own schedule, and
those differ by more than an order of magnitude — the Port Authority feeds
behind JFK, EWR and LGA update far less often than most. `n_scrapes` in `waits`
is the honest signal: a high value means the reading sat unchanged, not that the
queue was stable. Do not read sub-refresh-interval structure as real, and be
careful comparing volatility across airports — you may be measuring publishing
cadence rather than queues.

**Estimated values.** ~12,000 rows arrived as free text (`Greater than 30 minutes`, `Less than 5 mins`, `At least 30 mins`) rather than a number. These
were converted at collection time — lower bound for "greater than", midpoint for
"less than" — and flagged with `wait_is_estimated = true`. `status_raw` always
preserves the original. Drop them if you need exact readings.

**`closed` and `unknown`.** 4.0M rows report a closed checkpoint and 1.0M carry
no usable status. Both are kept, because checkpoint closures are themselves
interesting, but `wait_minutes` is meaningless for them. Filter on `is_open`.

**Terminal identifiers are the airport's own** and are not consistent between
airports. Some terminals expose several physical checkpoints under one label, so
multiple readings share a terminal and timestamp. **EWR Terminal B is the**
**clearest case**: the operator reports three checkpoints under the single label
"B", so each tick carries three rows per queue type with differing waits. Average
them, or treat the set as the terminal's spread — do not assume one is canonical.

**Gaps happen.** Upstream outages, deploys and network failures all leave holes.
There is no backfill and no interpolation — a missing interval is simply absent.

## Provenance

Collected by the scraper behind [nycwaittimes.com](https://nycwaittimes.com/),
which polls each airport's public wait-time feed roughly once a minute and
appends to Postgres. Every source is the airport operator's own public endpoint
— the Port Authority of NY & NJ for JFK/EWR/LGA, and each other operator's
public feed or published page — plus U.S. Customs and Border Protection's public
airport wait-time service for the `cbp` subset. There is no official nationwide
TSA feed; each airport is integrated separately.

A few operators publish no timestamp of their own. For those, notably **DTW**,
`reported_at_local` records when the reading was collected rather than when the
airport says it was measured. Everywhere else it is the operator's own stamp.

No personal data is collected. Every value is an aggregate the operator already
publishes to the travelling public.

Files are regenerated by `scripts/export-open-data.sh`, which pins a single
snapshot timestamp so all four subsets describe one consistent moment. That
timestamp is recorded in `snapshot.json`.
